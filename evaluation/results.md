# Evaluation results

Generated by `python -m evaluation.evaluate` on 2026-10-09.

- **Dataset:** `UpdatedResumeDataSet.csv`, 962 rows but only **166 unique resumes** once exact copies are removed, in 25 job categories
- **Jobs:** 25 job descriptions, one per category (`evaluation/job_descriptions/`)
- **Model:** `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, which reads at most 256 tokens at a time
- **Resumes longer than 256 tokens:** 107 of 166 (64%); median length 370 tokens
- **Share of all resume text the first version never showed the model:** 55%

| Method | Top-1 accuracy | Top-3 accuracy | R-precision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Keywords only - TF-IDF (second notebook) | 87.3% | 95.2% | 82.7% |
| 2. Meaning only, whole text - first 256 tokens (first version) | 67.5% | 83.7% | 61.9% |
| 3. Meaning only, 150-word chunks - full resume | 79.5% | 89.2% | 70.1% |
| 4. Meaning + keywords (this tool's default) | 89.2% | 97.6% | 81.2% |

- **Top-1:** the best-matching of the 25 jobs is the resume's true category.
- **Top-3:** the true category is among the 3 best-matching jobs.
- **R-precision:** for a job that R resumes truly belong to, the share of the top R ranked resumes that really belong to it, averaged over the 25 jobs.

With 166 resumes, one resume is 0.6%, so differences of a point or two are not meaningful.
